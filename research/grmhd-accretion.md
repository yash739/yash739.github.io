## Overview

Matter falling onto a black hole or neutron star forms an accretion disk, and how that disk sheds angular momentum decides how fast it accretes and how jets are launched. This was my senior thesis project with Prof. Rahul Kashyap at IIT Bombay.

## What I did

I used AthenaK to simulate accretion disks around compact objects across a range of length scales, including a check that the results are scale invariant. The main focus was the 3-D magneto-rotational instability and how it drives angular momentum transport.

I then tried to connect the simulations to observations across multiple scales, to learn about disk and jet structure, and used X-ray binaries to test predictions of strong gravity near rotating black holes.

## Related coursework

For Advanced Astrophysics (Jan to Apr 2025, also with Prof. Kashyap), I looked at thin accretion disks. I simulated angular momentum transport with 1-D non-linear diffusion equations for different viscosity profiles, used spectral features and accretion rates to tell neutron stars from black holes, and compared the timing of quasi-periodic oscillations with the relativistic predictions for the ISCO, periastron and Lense-Thirring frequencies.
